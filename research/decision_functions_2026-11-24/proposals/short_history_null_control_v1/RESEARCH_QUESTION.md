# Can conservative interaction preserve signal while controlling null risk

Target: P(a | question, admissible alternatives, observed context, own history). Immediate question: at k=16, can one global penalty pair and probability-blend weight retain strong personal interaction while limiting additional expected query loss relative to a development-selected common personal-offset comparator?

The study tests a fixed20-setting family, not architecture search. Development contains12 independent generated worlds. One selected setting and one common B3 penalty are frozen before confirmation. Confirmation has24–96 worlds under a predeclared precision rule, or stops before confirmation if none is eligible or the budget cannot meet projected precision.

The claim concerns average risk over the specified generated-world distribution, including shared fitting and training/support response variation. It does not guarantee every person, history, world, unseen domain, or null mechanism is safe. The two declared primary nulls must each pass. Known shared logits and two informative dimensions remain privileged synthetic conditions.

B3 may overfit. Candidate-minus-B3 bounds concern incremental interaction risk. Candidate-minus-known-shared-base and B3-minus-known-shared-base distributions are mandatory, but no source-blind noninferiority gate is added here. Thus a successful result must not be described as overall safe personalization. The known shared-base predictor is a strong, history-blind control; in regimes with offsets/interactions it is not necessarily the exact Bayes population marginal.

Strong-signal usefulness requires mean gain>=.05, recovery>=.50, contrast NRMSE<=.50, positive one-sided gain lower bound, and w>0. This excludes a B3 fallback. Weak, hidden nontransfer and mixed conditions are descriptive sensitivity tests and cannot rescue or veto the primary result.
